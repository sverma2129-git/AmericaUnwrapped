About this site

How we decide what’s true

America Unwrapped is a history site for high school students. This page explains how it’s written and checked, so you can decide how far to trust it.

Where the facts come from

Each chapter is built from the records of the time, such as cables, letters, treaties, speeches and photographs, and from books and articles by historians. A big claim has a small number after it. Click the number to see the source, what it supports, and what it doesn’t prove.

We put words in quotation marks only when we have checked them word for word against the original. If we couldn’t check a quotation, we say it in our own words and cite where it comes from. Right now 24 quotations on the site have been checked this way.

Settled facts and open arguments

Some things are settled. On the night of August 4, 1964, no North Vietnamese boats attacked the American destroyers in the Gulf of Tonkin, and we say so plainly.

Other questions are still argued by serious historians: whether the war could have been won, or whether Kennedy meant to pull out. For those, we show you the evidence each side uses and leave the judgment to you.

Washington, Hanoi and Saigon get the same hard look. We judge people by what they knew at the time, not by what we know now, and we try not to sort them into heroes and villains.

How the site is made

We use AI tools to help build and check the site. They are never a source of history. The facts come from archives and published scholarship, and every quotation is checked against the original.

There are no ads, trackers or accounts. Nothing about you is collected, and the pages are plain files, so they load quickly on a school Chromebook.

If you find a mistake, tell us. We’ll check it against the sources, fix it, and note what changed.
