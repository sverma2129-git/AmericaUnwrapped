The Vietnam War

How did thirty years of decisions lead to a war nobody could end?

From 1945 to 1975, six American presidents, the government in Saigon and the leaders in Hanoi each wanted something the others would not accept. Follow the war in 12 chapters, with maps, the original cables and practice questions for every chapter.

The chapters

In order, from Ho Chi Minh’s declaration of independence to how historians argue about the war today. Each chapter asks one question and answers it with the evidence.

  1. 1Chapter 1, 1945–1954, a turning pointDien Bien Phu and the end of French ruleWhy did the United States pay for 80% of France’s war in Indochina, and why did the French fortress at Dien Bien Phu fall?
  2. 2Chapter 2, 1954–1960Geneva splits Vietnam in twoWhy were the elections promised at Geneva never held, and how did the war in the South begin?
  3. 3Chapter 3, 1961–1963Kennedy, Diem and the coupHow did Kennedy’s plan to fight guerrillas fail at Ap Bac, and why did Washington back the coup that killed Ngo Dinh Diem?
  4. 4Chapter 4, 1964, a turning pointThe Gulf of TonkinWhat really happened in the Gulf of Tonkin in August 1964, and how did it lead to a much larger American war?
  5. 5Chapter 5, 1965–1967America takes over the warWhy did General Westmoreland’s plan to wear down the enemy fail to break Hanoi’s will to fight?
  6. 6Chapter 6, 1961–1972The secret warWhat did America’s hidden war in Laos and South Vietnam achieve, and what did it cost?
  7. 7Chapter 7, 1968, a turning pointThe Tet OffensiveHow did a military defeat for the Viet Cong become the turning point of the war for the United States?
  8. 8Chapter 8, 1969–1972Nixon and VietnamizationWas Nixon’s "Vietnamization" a real plan for South Vietnam to stand alone, or a way to buy a "decent interval" before it fell?
  9. 9Chapter 9, 1972–1973, a turning pointThe Paris Peace AccordsWhy did Nixon order the Christmas Bombing of 1972, and why did the 1973 Paris Peace Accords fail to end the war?
  10. 10Chapter 10, 1973–1975, a turning pointThe fall of SaigonSouth Vietnam’s army was one of the best-equipped in the world on paper. Why did it collapse in just 55 days in the spring of 1975?
  11. 11Chapter 11, 1975–1995After the warWhat did thirty years of war cost Vietnam’s people and land, and how did two former enemies make peace?
  12. 12Chapter 12, The ArgumentHow historians argue about the warWas the Vietnam War a quagmire that could never be won, or a war the U.S. could have won if politicians and the press had not gotten in the way?
