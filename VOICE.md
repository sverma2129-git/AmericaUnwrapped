# How America Unwrapped writes

The reader is a 16-year-old who has a test coming and would rather be doing something else.
Write so that student understands on the first read. The tests in
`src/data/__tests__/contentIntegrity.test.ts` enforce the parts of this guide a machine can check.

## The voice

Plain and direct, a little warm. Friendly in buttons and quiz feedback. Serious about the war:
no jokes about the history.

## Rules

1. **Write to one student.** Use "you". Use contractions.
2. **Use the everyday word.** "Plan", not "strategic imperative". "Sources", not "archival dossier".
3. **Headings say what happened or ask the real question.** Sentence case. No ampersands. No
   "Label: Subtitle" unless the subtitle adds a fact.
4. **One idea per sentence.** Aim for under 20 words. Never over 30.
5. **Name the person, place, number and date.** Cut adjectives that pass judgment for the
   reader: catastrophic, pivotal, definitive, brutal, fatal.
6. **One name per thing, everywhere.**

   | Say | Not |
   |---|---|
   | chapter | inquiry, dossier |
   | study guide | master study guide, exam review |
   | quick summary | briefing, 60-second briefing |
   | sources | dossier, archival record, citations |
   | practice quiz | self-quiz, assessment, concept check |
   | key terms | vocabulary dossier, glossary deck |

7. **Buttons name what happens next.** "Next chapter", "Check answer", "See the source".
8. **Never reword a quotation.** Facts, numbers, names and dates never change in a rewrite.

## Words we don't use in headings, labels or summaries

forensic, dossier, archival, ecosystem, imperative, matrix, anatomy, calculus, paradigm,
historiographical (say "how historians argue"), high-yield, master (as in "master guide"),
definitive, cataclysmic, pivotal, tactical and strategic when "plan" or "military" will do.

"Doctrine" is fine in a real name (the Nixon Doctrine, the Truman Doctrine).

## Checks before publishing

- Read it aloud. If you would not say it to a classmate, rewrite it.
- No em dashes. Use a period or a comma.
- No lists of three for rhythm. Two items, or the real number.
- Reading level at or below grade 11 (the tests measure this).

## Where this comes from

Duolingo's voice guidelines (simple words, supportive and clear, talks to the learner), and two
open-source editing checklists: stop-slop (Hardik Pandya) and humanizer (blader, built on
Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing").
